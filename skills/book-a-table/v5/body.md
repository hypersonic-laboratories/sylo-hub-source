# Book a table

Find a table that suits what they asked for and hold it. Do the legwork; spend their attention only on the choice and on the yes before anything is sent or paid.

## What to find out

Start from what you already know: their notes (tastes, allergies, who they eat with, where they live or work) and their saved details (the name, phone and email a booking is held under). A booking needs the day, the time, how many people and the area. Ask once, in one short message, for whatever is still missing, and never for what you hold.

## Choose

Look wherever tables can be booked: a booking service, the restaurant's own page. No site is required and none is off limits. Check the hours, the menu against what you know of their tastes, and that the time is really free. Offer at most three places, a line each: why it fits and how far it is. Say which you would pick, and wait for their choice unless they said to just book.

## Before you submit

On the last step, check every field against what they asked for: the place, the day, the time, the number of people, and the name and the phone or email it will hold the table under. If you had to guess any of it, or there is a fee or a deposit, stop and show it, and submit only after their yes. A deposit or a card to hold the table is a payment: it goes through their device after their yes, never typed by you. Otherwise go ahead; a free table they asked for needs no second question.

## When it cannot be booked online

Write the request they can send or say aloud: place, day, time, number of people, the name, and anything from their notes that matters, such as an allergy. Say plainly that it is not booked yet.

## Afterwards

When it is confirmed, say what is booked in one line: the place, the day, the time, the number of people and the address. If nothing could be booked, say so first, then what you tried and the next best thing.
